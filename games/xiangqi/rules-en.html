Xiangqi (Chinese Chess) Rules: The Horse Leg, Elephant Eye, and Flying General

2026-11-09 · 8 min read

Xiangqi（中国象棋）, or Chinese chess, has only seven piece types and 90 points on the board. The rules still catch Western players out, because three pieces can be blocked in ways that have no equivalent in international chess: the horse's leg, the elephant's eye, and the cannon's screen. Learn those three ideas and the rest follows quickly.

The board and the starting position

The board has 9 vertical files and 10 horizontal ranks. Pieces sit on the intersections, not inside the squares, giving 90 playable points.

A river（河界）runs across the middle between the fifth and sixth ranks. Each side has a palace（九宫）, a 3×3 box marked with diagonals, at its end of the board. The generals and advisors may not leave the palace.

Each side starts with 16 pieces: one general, two advisors, two elephants, two horses, two chariots, two cannons, and five soldiers. Red sets up at the bottom and moves first. Black sets up at the top.

How each piece moves

Chariot（车）

The chariot moves any number of points horizontally or vertically, as long as no piece blocks the path. It cannot jump. It captures by landing on an enemy piece. This is the strongest piece on the board, and it needs open lines to be useful.

Horse（马）

The horse moves one point straight and then one point diagonally, the shape of the Chinese character 日. It is the only piece with a blocking rule in the middle of its move.

The straight point is called the horse leg（马腿）. If any piece, friendly or enemy, occupies that point, the horse cannot move in that direction. Both destinations on that side are blocked. This is why a horse in the open center has eight possible moves, while a horse at the edge may have only two or three.

Example. A horse on the bottom rank wants to jump up and left. The point directly above it is occupied. The horse cannot make that jump, even though the landing point is empty. The blocking piece does not get captured or moved; it simply stops the horse.

Cannon（炮）

When a cannon is not capturing, it moves exactly like a chariot: any distance horizontally or vertically, no jumping.

When a cannon captures, it must jump over exactly one piece. That piece is the cannon screen（炮架）. The screen may be friendly or enemy. The cannon lands on the first enemy piece beyond the screen and captures it. With no screen, the cannon cannot capture. With two or more pieces in between, it also cannot capture.

This makes the cannon a positional weapon. A cannon aimed down an open file is harmless until a screen appears. A cannon aimed at a general with one piece in between is a constant threat.

Elephant（象 / 相）

The elephant moves exactly two points diagonally, tracing the character 田. The midpoint of that move is the elephant eye（象眼）. If any piece occupies the eye, the elephant cannot move in that direction.

An elephant may not cross the river. It stays on its own half of the board for the whole game and works as a defender around the palace.

Advisor（士 / 仕）

The advisor moves one point diagonally and may never leave the palace. It is a bodyguard for the general and blocks lines of attack through the palace.

General（帅 / 将）

The general moves one point horizontally or vertically and may never leave the palace. It cannot move into check.

There is also the flying general rule（飞将）. The two generals may not face each other on the same file with no piece between them. If a move would create that position, the move is illegal. The general is effectively attacking the other general along the open file, and a player may not expose their own general that way.

Soldier（兵 / 卒）

Before crossing the river, a soldier moves one point forward only. It cannot move sideways or backward.

After crossing the river, a soldier may move one point forward or one point sideways. It still cannot move backward. A soldier that reaches the enemy palace area is a serious attacking piece, because it restricts the general's movement.

Capture, check and checkmate

You capture by moving onto an enemy piece's point and removing it. There is no special capture rule apart from the cannon's screen requirement.

A general is in check when an enemy piece could capture it on the next move. The player in check must answer immediately: capture the attacker, block the line, or move the general to a safe point.

If the general is in check and no legal move removes the check, it is checkmate（将死）, and that player loses.

Xiangqi also treats stalemate（困毙）as a loss. If the player to move is not in check but has no legal move at all, that player loses. This differs from international chess, where stalemate is a draw.

This implementation ends the game when the side to move has no legal move. It does not declare a separate draw for repetition or for a long sequence of checks; those are tournament conventions rather than part of the board engine here.

Opening principles that actually help

  1. Develop the chariots and horses early. A chariot on an open file controls more points than a chariot stuck behind its own soldiers. A horse near the center has more legs available than a horse on the edge.
  2. Do not trade pieces for no reason. Early exchanges simplify the position for both sides. Unless you gain a clear attack or material advantage, keep your pieces on the board.
  3. Secure the palace first. The center file and the palace are the main highways of attack. Fix your advisors and elephants before launching a flank attack.
  4. Use the cannon's screen. A cannon is strongest when a screen already sits on its line. Look for screens your opponent creates, and avoid leaving a valuable piece as the only screen in front of your general.
  5. Watch the open file. Before moving an advisor, elephant, chariot or cannon away from the center file, check whether that opens a line between the generals or exposes your palace.

The three mistakes beginners make

1. Ignoring the horse leg. A player sees an empty landing point and moves the horse, not noticing that the straight point is blocked. Before any horse move, check the leg.
2. Treating the cannon like a chariot. A cannon cannot capture without a screen. Seeing a clear line to an enemy piece is not enough; there must be exactly one piece in between.
3. Forgetting the flying general. A piece on the center file is moved aside, and the two generals suddenly face each other. The move is illegal. Check the center file before moving any piece that stands between the generals.

Common questions

What is the horse leg in xiangqi?

A horse moves one square straight and then one square diagonally. The straight square is the horse leg. If any piece occupies that square, both destinations in that direction are blocked, even though the horse would jump over it in a game like chess.

Does a cannon need a screen to capture?

Yes. When a cannon captures, there must be exactly one piece between the cannon and its target. That piece is the screen, and it may belong to either side. With no screen, or with two or more pieces in between, the cannon cannot capture.

Can the two generals face each other?

No. The two generals may not stand on the same file with no piece between them. A move that creates this position is illegal, because the general would be attacking the other general along the open file. This is the flying general rule.
