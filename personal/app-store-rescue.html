Scope and limits

Clear on both sides before any money moves.

What is covered

  • Guideline 2.1 — app completeness, crashes, unfinished or stub content, reviewer access
  • Guideline 4.2 — minimum functionality and web-wrapper rejects
  • Guideline 4.3 — spam, duplicate apps, and templated submissions
  • Guideline 5.1 — privacy disclosures, permissions, tracking, account deletion
  • Metadata, screenshots, previews, age rating, and export compliance

Not charged

If the initial review shows the problem is not fixable within the tier — for example a fundamentally incomplete app, a business model Apple prohibits, or content that cannot be remedied — I decline the engagement before payment.

If a case turns out to be out of scope after kickoff, I stop and you are not charged for the remainder. No percentage claims, no approval guarantee.

On approval: Apple makes the final decision. I do not promise that an app will pass, and I do not quote approval rates. What I do is remove every concrete reason reviewers cite and put the submission in the strongest position I can.

FAQ

The questions that come up before a first email.

Which rejection reasons do you handle?

Most commonly guideline 2.1 (App Completeness), 4.2 (Minimum Functionality), 4.3 (Spam), and 5.1 (Privacy). Metadata rejects, missing account deletion, sign-in problems for reviewers, misleading screenshots, and incomplete demo accounts are also covered. If the rejection falls outside what I can fix, I say so before any payment.

What do I need to provide?

The original rejection text from App Store Connect, including the full Resolution Center thread and any screenshots Apple attached; the build or a TestFlight link with reviewer access; the current metadata and privacy answers; and your target release date. If you have previous rejections on the same app, include those too.

Is the work remote?

Yes. Everything is remote and asynchronous over email and shared documents, with one 60-minute call included in every tier for questions on the report. I can work from a TestFlight build, a screen recording, or repository access if you prefer to grant it.

How does payment work?

50% at kickoff and 50% after the app is approved. The tier price is fixed, so there is no hourly billing or surprise invoice. If I determine during the initial review that the problem is outside what I can fix, I do not take the engagement and nothing is charged.

Can you guarantee approval?

No. Apple makes the final decision, and no consultant can guarantee it. What I can do is remove the concrete reasons reviewers cite and put the submission in a much stronger position: apps that ship the fixes on the list usually clear review at a notably higher rate.

How is this different from the $750 performance audit?

Rejection Rescue fixes what blocks App Store review: guideline violations, metadata gaps, reviewer access, and the resolution-center reply. The performance audit measures launch time, memory, and frame cost, and ends with a prioritized optimization backlog. Both are $750 fixed, but they are separate scopes; one does not include the other.
